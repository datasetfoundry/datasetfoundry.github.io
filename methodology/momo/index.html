Methodology · Mobile Money

What ClaimsForge's Mobile Money line actually does

This page is meant to be citable directly in a methodology chapter. Everything below is a design decision, not a discovery — ClaimsForge does not model real Ghanaian telco or transaction data, it encodes a documented set of assumptions about how Mobile Money fraud-signal factors should relate to fraud outcomes, so the relationships can be recovered and used to teach or test fraud-detection methods. This is the first non-insurance line on the platform, and it deliberately does not mirror the motor, life, health, or Home & Property lines' two-stage frequency/severity model — see section 1 for why.

1. Single-stage model

Every record is one simulated Mobile Money transaction, generated in a single stage rather than the two-stage frequency/severity split every insurance line on this platform uses. Fraud labeling has no severity-equivalent to model separately: a transaction was either fraudulent or it wasn't, and how much money moved is a property of the transaction itself, not of the fraud outcome, so transaction_amount_ghs is drawn independently of is_fraudulent rather than conditioned on it the way, say, a claim's cost is conditioned on a claim having occurred. Fraud probability is a logistic (log-odds) model over four independent risk factors that combine additively, closer to motor's from-scratch logistic frequency model than to life's or health's table-rated approach — table-rating doesn't fit here because there's no single natural ordinal axis, like age, to band against.

Alongside the binary is_fraudulent label, every record also carries a continuous anomaly_score. This is not a second coefficient table or a second hypothesis set — it's derived from the same underlying fraud logit plus independent noise, so a downstream fraud-detection model has a continuous score to threshold against, rather than only the binary ground-truth label it's meant to be trained to predict.

2. The four hypotheses

All four hypotheses act on fraud probability directly — there's no `claim_occurred`-style gate to condition a second-stage check on the way an insurance line's severity hypotheses are conditioned on a claim having occurred. Each row below is independently documented against publicly-known Mobile Money fraud typologies (Bank of Ghana and GSMA mobile-money fraud advisories), not invented to hit a target fraud rate.

FactorDirection on fraud probabilityVerification n
Transaction velocity (trailing 24h) Positive 20,000
SIM-swap recency Positive 20,000
Wallet tenure Positive (inverse — newer wallets carry more risk) 20,000
Agent cash-out channel Positive (Cash-Out rows only) 20,000

Transaction velocity — a rapidly rising count of transactions in the trailing 24 hours is the structuring/"smurfing" signal: splitting or cycling funds through many small transactions to move stolen money before a victim or the telco can freeze the wallet, or to stay under a reporting threshold. SIM-swap recency is by far the largest single loading in the model — a fresh SIM swap (within the last 24 hours) is widely documented as the strongest individual fraud signal in the Mobile Money literature this line is anchored against, and the loading decays as the swap ages out of that highest-risk window. Wallet tenure works inversely: a wallet opened within the last month has no established legitimate transaction history to compare against and is disproportionately the vehicle a fraud ring picks, so the loading is highest for the newest band and actually goes slightly negative for the oldest ("3+ years") band, rather than just flattening at zero. Agent cash-out channel only applies to Cash-Out rows (about 8% of all transactions) — an unmanned ATM cash-out carries the largest loading of the four channels since it leaves the weakest human trail, while a Bank Partner Cash-Out sits below the reference band, since it inherits the bank's own KYC checks. Because this hypothesis is only measurable on Cash-Out rows and carries the weakest of the four loadings, it needs more generated records than the other three to hold consistently — see the note on verification n below.

All four hypotheses are verified at n=20,000, not the n=5,000 the motor/life/health/property lines default to. This isn't a conservative buffer applied uniformly — it's driven specifically by the agent cash-out channel hypothesis: at n=5,000 (roughly 400 Cash-Out rows per seed), the weak, Cash-Out-only channel effect flips sign for at least one regression seed, sampling noise on a real-but-weak effect rather than a broken hypothesis. At n=20,000 (roughly 1,600 Cash-Out rows per seed) it holds positive across every regression seed, so all four hypotheses are reported at that n for consistency, not just the one that strictly needs it.

3. Coefficients

Fraud probability is modeled as p = clip(sigmoid(intercept + velocity_band_loading + agent_channel_loading + sim_swap_loading + wallet_tenure_loading), min_probability, max_probability), where each loading is a named log-odds term keyed off the transaction's own velocity band, agent cash-out channel (only applied on Cash-Out rows — zero everywhere else), SIM-swap recency band, and wallet tenure band. The intercept is tuned so the realized fraud rate across a generated dataset lands in the low single-digit percent range, consistent with Mobile Money fraud-detection literature treating fraud as a rare-event classification problem, not a guess to hit an arbitrary target. anomaly_score is derived from that same logit plus independent normal noise (anomaly_score_noise_sd), then passed back through the sigmoid and clipped to [0, 1] — it is correlated with, but not a deterministic function of, is_fraudulent, by construction. Exact magnitudes aren't published on this page and aren't derived from real Ghanaian telco data — see the current defaults, and change them, in the Advanced: customize the model panel on the Mobile Money generator if your thesis calls for weaker or stronger effects, and note in your write-up which values you changed from the defaults.

4. Determinism

Generation runs server-side using a seeded generator (numpy's default_rng, PCG64) rather than an unseeded source of randomness — same mechanism as every other line on the platform. The same seed, record count, and generator version (stamped in every citation and manifest — see the Cite section on the generator page) will always reproduce byte-identical output. Report the seed and record count alongside your dataset — that pair, together with the generator version, is sufficient for anyone to regenerate exactly what you analyzed.

5. What's decorative vs. load-bearing

Region and transaction amount exist to make records look like real Ghanaian Mobile Money transactions and are internally consistent with each other, but they are not the object of the four hypotheses above — transaction_amount_ghs is drawn from a per-transaction-type range independently of the fraud outcome (section 1), not as a driver of it. Unlike every insurance line on this platform, momo records carry no subscriber name field at all: a Mobile Money fraud log is keyed off a wallet/MSISDN identifier, not a name, so there is no name-inclusion toggle to disable here (contrast the other four lines' insured_name, gated by the name-inclusion toggle — ROADMAP.md Phase 4.5.3).

6. Known limitations

  • This is synthetic data. It should never be presented as, or mistaken for, real telco or transaction data in a publication.
  • The four risk factors combine additively in log-odds space, with no interaction effects between transaction velocity, SIM-swap recency, wallet tenure, and agent cash-out channel modeled beyond that simple addition, unless you add them yourself downstream.
  • The agent cash-out channel hypothesis is only measurable on Cash-Out rows (about 8% of all transactions) — generating fewer than a few thousand records will make that specific comparison look noisy even though the other three hold up at smaller n.
  • If your thesis needs a different balance between the four hypotheses, adjust the relevant loadings in the Advanced: customize the model panel on the Mobile Money generator and say so in your write-up.

7. Citing this tool

Something like: "Synthetic data generated using ClaimsForge (seed = 42, n = 20,000), a deterministic Mobile Money fraud-signal data generator built for this thesis in the absence of accessible real-world MoMo transaction data." Include the seed and record count so the exact dataset is reproducible from the tool alone — or use the auto-generated citation block on the generator page, which also includes the generator version and any advanced-config changes.
